# Descriptive anatomy notes

Status: literature notes, not measurements of the prospective partner's plants.

## Whole plant and leaves

Flora of North America describes an upright annual herb with a taproot, variable branching, and palmately compound leaves. “Palmately compound” means several leaflets belong to one leaf, radiating from a common region at the end of its petiole. A leaflet should not be confused with a separate stem branch. [FNA-CANNABIS](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=105522)

Flora of Pakistan describes long petioles, lower leaves arranged oppositely and upper leaves alternately, and two slender lateral stipules. This is a botanical description, not a guarantee that every specimen or photographed node displays every feature. [FOP-CANNABIS](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=5&taxon_id=105522)

The species entry describes narrow, pointed leaflets with toothed margins and different surface hair characteristics above and below. It reports a range of leaflet counts; a single seven-leaflet outline is not a universal anatomical template. [FOP-SATIVA](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=5&taxon_id=200006342)

## Tissue scale

Nakkliang and colleagues examined leaf morphology and microscopy for botanical identification. Their indexed text describes epidermal surfaces, hairs, and sections through the midrib and leaf blade, identifying palisade and spongy tissue, vascular tissue, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma. These are anatomical observations; they are not measurements of fresh tissue stiffness or damage thresholds. Full figures still require inspection. [LEAF-2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9355051/)

## Vocabulary for reading references

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Node / internode | Stem attachment region / stem segment between successive nodes |
| Petiole | Stalk connecting a leaf to the stem |
| Leaflet | One division of a compound leaf |
| Lamina | Broad leaf or leaflet blade |
| Midrib | Main central vein of a blade |
| Adaxial / abaxial | Faces toward / away from the stem axis; commonly the upper / lower leaf surfaces |
| Trichome | Epidermal hair or outgrowth |

These terms distinguish anatomical structures. They do not by themselves specify how those structures deform or fail.
