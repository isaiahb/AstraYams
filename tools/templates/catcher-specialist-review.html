Task and reward proposal · Run data · Demonstration manifest

What Astra shaped

Reward approaching the target and keeping the catcher upright.
Penalize abrupt commands and unexpected contact.
Require both jaws to retain the tool.
Finish only after 0.4 seconds continuously inside the tolerances.

Training varies plant position, yaw and stiffness. The final checkpoint is selected on development seeds. A separate untouched seed set is then evaluated using the same fixed criteria.

Why the completion reward changed

Later policies could collect more discounted reward by hovering near the target than by finishing the required hold. Astra compared a passing and a failing development rollout: 41.0 versus 81.5 discounted return. The next reward revision increased completion bonus from 12 to 200 while keeping every success threshold unchanged.

What the policy controls and what remains engineered

The learned network produces six joint-target increments at 50 Hz. Finite-torque PD controllers drive the simulated motors. IK was used once to construct a constant starting posture, not to choose execution movements. The jaw controller is engineered; PPO controls the arm.

All plant joints remain dynamic. Plant properties and actuator behavior are uncalibrated; robot self-collision is disabled in this local trial. The catcher frame and handle have rigid contacts. Its textile is a visual rest shape; this task stops before fruit/net contact. Recorded trials do not establish real-arm transfer or bruise safety.

Successful state/action trajectories are synthetic demonstrations. Training a camera-based student from rendered demonstrations is a separate next step. This page presents the actual current run; it is not yet a general prompt-to-training application.
