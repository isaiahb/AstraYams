# Photo reconstruction: carriage and side bracing

The new front, side and carriage photographs show a narrow vertical lift on a
deep floor rectangle. Four stays form triangles in two side planes. V2's rear X
and broad arm shelf did not describe that apparatus. V3 replaces them with the
observed arrangement and retains the V2 growing tower and V4 plant mechanics.

## Evidence and dimensional interpretation

User photos, kept locally in the ignored run folder:

- `PXL_20260911_030015167.jpg`: full frontal arrangement.
- `PXL_20260911_030001146.jpg`: compact carriage faceplate, motor crossbeam,
  outer posts and two central guides.
- `PXL_20260911_030032435.jpg`: base depth and fore/aft side stays.
- `PXL_20260911_030038355.jpg`: green guide blocks, circular black component,
  side stays and floor joints.

The user authorized estimating from their earlier rough “6 ft” and “4 ft”
figures. The working interpretation is **6 ft to the working crossbeam, about
2 ft across, 4 ft front-to-back**, with outer posts extending higher. This
reassigns the earlier 4 ft width to depth based on the side photographs. It is
an inference, not a corrected measurement or calibrated photogrammetry result.

## What is modeled

- Four slotted extrusion stays terminate on the outer uprights and fore/aft
  floor rails; no rear X.
- Tall outer posts continue above the working crossbeam.
- A motor and gearhead envelope sit above the crossbeam, beside a vertical belt.
- Four green channel blocks surround the two central guides.
- A black compact faceplate includes holes and visible fastener heads.
- The round black component is a separate CAD solid. A through-shaft and spacer
  provide an explicit connection to the plate; these internal mounting parts are
  a hypothesis. Their exact geometry and retention are not verified hardware.
- The photographs do not identify the motor, gearbox, belt pitch, bearings,
  payload purpose, bolt pitch, shaft retention or actual structural capacity.

The default **rig** model has one lift actuator and the photographed round
component. **SO-101** and **YAM** alternatives replace that component with a
separately labeled proposed bridge, brackets, adapters and two actual robot
models. The rig model contains no hidden robot arms. These proposed mounts are
layout studies, not manufacturing-ready or load-rated retrofits.

Each modeled support part is a parametric CAD solid with STEP/STL exports. The
support assembly STEP is built from the same poses as the simulation. Existing
V1, V2 and YAM harvesting checkpoints remain available.

## Physics scope

Lift motion is generated by MuJoCo's finite-force position actuator and replayed
from recorded states. The script rate-limits the commanded height to 0.08 m/s;
350 N is an unmeasured force prior. The explicit carriage inertia is a 2.8 kg
prior; moving child collision shapes and robot inertias can add mass. Therefore
the complete moving assembly is not simply a measured 2.8 kg payload.

The guides are represented by a prismatic constraint. The frame is fixed and
rigid. This does not model belt elasticity, tooth engagement, bearing preload,
voltage/current/thermal limits, braking on power loss, frame deflection or
tipping. Many small fasteners and inferred shaft parts are visual geometry;
contact uses documented coarse envelopes. The review's contact number measures
robot contacts only and cannot certify whole-cell structural clearance.

This is a scripted lift commissioning test. It has no harvesting reward, PPO
training, fruit-damage guarantee or hardware-capacity validation.

## Acceptance evidence

`tests/test_lift_rig.py` checks actual generated member endpoints in the two side
planes; distinct rig/arm physical models; valid connected CAD parts; a clear
20 mm guide channel; and downward motion when lift force is disabled despite
an upward command. Recorded-force checks enforce finite values, bounded forces,
nonzero travel and no simulator warnings.

The review compares photos with front, side, carriage and perspective renders.
The lift is inspected at its initial, raised and returned poses. Arm variants
have separate full-workcell views. Exact results and source hashes are recorded
in `tower-lift-v3-evidence.json` after commissioning.

## Remaining discrepancies, ranked

1. **Dimensional uncertainty:** confirm one horizontal and one vertical span,
   extrusion section, guide spacing and carriage bolt pattern before designing
   a physical retrofit. Present estimates, not false precision.
2. **Mounting and drive uncertainty:** identify the round component and actual
   guide mechanism; measure moving mass, stroke and drive limits. The inferred
   shaft/spacer and bridge need mechanical interface and capacity verification.
3. **Robot workspace:** the parked SO-101 gripper lightly brushes a modeled leaf
   during the lift. This is logged contact, not a collision-free pass. It needs a
   stow/approach policy before using this lift traversal for harvesting.
4. **Appearance:** the source robot meshes still show some shading artifacts in
   the cel renderer. The corrected rig geometry is reviewable, but photometric
   matching and supplier-level motor/bearing detail remain unfinished.
5. **Tower/plant fit:** the newer pocket closeups can refine the top rim and
   internal ribs later. V2 was retained here to keep the carriage comparison
   focused. Root compliance, biological growth and damage remain uncalibrated.

## Reproduce locally

```sh
PYTHONPATH=src /tmp/clonebench-cad-env/bin/python tools/build_tower_lift.py --config configs/tower_lift_v3.json --out runs/tower-lift-v3
PYTHONPATH=src /tmp/clonebench-cad-env/bin/python tools/review_tower_lift.py --root runs/tower-lift-v3 --template tools/templates/rig-photo-review.html
PYTHONPATH=src /tmp/clonebench-cad-env/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_lift_rig.py' -v
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8793/runs/tower-lift-v3/index.html` with the existing local
server. Generated STEP/STL files, reference photos and replays remain outside Git.
